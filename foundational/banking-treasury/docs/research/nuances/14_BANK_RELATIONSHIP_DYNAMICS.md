# Bank Relationship & Negotiation Nuances

## Overview

Every treasury document covers "select a bank" but none cover "how to actually negotiate with banks." Bank relationships are built on leverage, data, and mutual value—not just product features. Understanding these dynamics is the difference between paying market rates and getting optimal terms.

---

## The Problem Most Research Ignores

Provider comparisons focus on capabilities, but operational reality involves:
- **Negotiation leverage** varies by relationship depth
- **Credit facility covenants** constrain treasury operations
- **Relationship pricing** vs. transactional pricing
- **Bank economics** that drive their behavior
- **Crisis management** when things go wrong
- **Internal bank dynamics** (RM vs. product specialist vs. credit)

---

## Bank Relationship Value Quantification

### The Leverage Equation

| Factor | Impact on Negotiating Power |
|--------|----------------------------|
| **Deposit balances** | High - Banks want sticky deposits |
| **Credit utilization** | Medium - Shows relationship value |
| **Transaction volume** | Medium - Fee revenue stream |
| **Ancillary business** | Medium - FX, trade finance, etc. |
| **Growth potential** | High - Banks value future revenue |
| **Switching costs** | Works both ways - leverage or lock-in |

### How Banks Measure Relationship Value

Banks calculate **Risk-Adjusted Return on Capital (RAROC)** for each relationship:

```
RAROC = (Revenue - Costs - Expected Loss) / Economic Capital

Where:
- Revenue = Interest margin + Fees + Float
- Costs = Operational costs + Cost of funds
- Expected Loss = Credit risk provision
- Economic Capital = Regulatory capital allocation
```

**Key Insight**: Credit facilities consume significant capital. Banks need fee income to justify relationships.

## Negotiation Strategies

### Understanding Bank Perspective

| Bank Need | Your Leverage |
|-----------|---------------|
| **Deposit balances** | Concentrate balances pre-negotiation |
| **Fee income** | Commit volume in exchange for rate |
| **Capital efficiency** | Offer collateral or guarantees |
| **Long-term relationship** | Multi-year commitments |

### Know Your BATNA

> "Knowing your BATNA (Best Alternative to a Negotiated Agreement) is essential. It empowers the treasurer with the knowledge of when to walk away."

**Example**: If Bank A won't reduce wire fees, having Bank B's quote as alternative creates real leverage.

**Source**: [FasterCapital Bank Relationship Management](https://www.fastercapital.com/content/Bank-Relationship-Management--Banking-on-Relationships--Managing-Partnerships-in-Treasury-Roles.html)

### Data-Driven Negotiation

| Data Point | How to Use |
|------------|------------|
| **Transaction volumes** | Demonstrate value, negotiate volume discounts |
| **Payment timing patterns** | Show predictability, reduce bank's risk |
| **Liquidity patterns** | Commit balances in exchange for pricing |
| **Competitive quotes** | Benchmark for negotiation |

**Source**: [Treasury Management International - Quantify It](https://treasury-management.com/articles/bank-relationship-management-quantify-it)

## Credit Facility Considerations

### Covenant Categories

| Covenant Type | Example | Treasury Impact |
|---------------|---------|-----------------|
| **Financial ratios** | Debt/EBITDA < 3.5x | Constrains borrowing decisions |
| **Minimum liquidity** | $10M unrestricted cash | Ties up working capital |
| **Restricted payments** | Dividend limits | Affects intercompany flows |
| **Negative pledge** | No other liens | Limits future financing |
| **Material adverse change** | Bank discretion | Crisis vulnerability |

### Covenant Negotiation Tips

1. **Push for cure periods** - Time to fix breaches
2. **Define terms precisely** - Avoid bank interpretation
3. **Build in headroom** - Don't set ratios at current levels
4. **Carve-out specific activities** - Permitted acquisitions, etc.
5. **Limit cross-default** - Isolate facility defaults

## Fee Negotiation Framework

### Common Treasury Fees

| Fee Type | Typical Range | Negotiation Potential |
|----------|---------------|----------------------|
| **Wire transfer (domestic)** | $10-30 | Medium - volume discounts |
| **Wire transfer (international)** | $25-50 | Medium - volume discounts |
| **ACH** | $0.10-0.50 | High - volume very elastic |
| **Account maintenance** | $20-100/month | Low - often waived for balances |
| **Cash management platform** | $500-5000/month | Medium - bundled pricing |
| **Positive pay** | $50-200/month | Low - security feature |

### Fee Reduction Strategies

1. **Bundle services** - Package pricing vs. à la carte
2. **Commit volume** - Guaranteed minimums for lower unit cost
3. **ECR (Earnings Credit Rate)** - Offset fees with balance credits
4. **Annual review** - Scheduled renegotiation points
5. **Competitive bidding** - RFP process every 3-5 years

## Consolidated vs. Multi-Bank Strategy

### Consolidated Relationship Benefits

| Benefit | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Relationship pricing** | Better rates with concentration |
| **Single point of contact** | Simpler communication |
| **Integrated solutions** | Systems work together |
| **Holistic advisory** | Bank sees full picture |

### Multi-Bank Strategy Benefits

| Benefit | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Competitive tension** | Better pricing through competition |
| **Redundancy** | Backup if one bank fails |
| **Specialized capabilities** | Best-in-class for each need |
| **Risk diversification** | Not dependent on one institution |

### Recommended Approach: Hybrid

```
Primary Bank (60-70% of business)
├── Main operating accounts
├── Primary credit facility
├── Core cash management
└── Relationship pricing

Secondary Bank (20-30% of business)
├── Backup operating accounts
├── Secondary credit facility
├── Competitive tension
└── Specialized products

Specialist Providers (10-20%)
├── Specific capabilities (FX, trade)
├── Regional expertise
└── Innovation access
```

**Source**: [UMB Consolidated Banking Benefits](https://blog.umb.com/business-banking-guide-the-benefits-of-one-business-bank-provider/)

## Internal Bank Dynamics

### Key Roles to Understand

| Role | Responsibility | How to Engage |
|------|----------------|---------------|
| **Relationship Manager (RM)** | Overall relationship, revenue target | Primary contact, strategic discussions |
| **Product Specialist** | Deep expertise in specific products | Technical questions, optimization |
| **Credit Officer** | Approve/decline credit facilities | Often invisible but powerful |
| **Operations** | Day-to-day transaction processing | Escalation for service issues |

### Effective Communication

1. **RM for strategic conversations** - Pricing, new products, relationship expansion
2. **Product specialist for optimization** - Technical improvements, best practices
3. **Operations for issues** - Service problems, transaction queries
4. **Credit for facility concerns** - Covenant discussions, amendments

## Crisis Communication

### When Things Go Wrong

| Situation | Recommended Action |
|-----------|-------------------|
| **Service disruption** | Document, escalate to RM, request credits |
| **Covenant breach** | Proactive communication, present remediation plan |
| **Payment failure** | Immediate escalation, timeline commitment |
| **Fraud/security incident** | Direct to security team, follow protocols |

### Building Goodwill for Crisis

1. **Regular communication** - Don't only call when there's a problem
2. **Transparency** - Share business plans and challenges
3. **Responsiveness** - Reply quickly to bank requests
4. **Fair dealing** - Honor commitments, even informal ones

## System Design Implications

### Required Capabilities

1. **Bank relationship database** - Contacts, products, pricing
2. **Fee tracking and analysis** - Actual vs. contracted
3. **Balance reporting** - Cross-bank visibility
4. **Covenant monitoring** - Automated ratio calculations
5. **RFP management** - Structured bank selection process
6. **Performance benchmarking** - Service quality metrics
