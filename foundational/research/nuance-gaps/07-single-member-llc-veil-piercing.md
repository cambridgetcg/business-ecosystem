# Single-Member LLC Veil Piercing Risks

## Knowledge Gap Identified

Current corporate veil research (06-corporate-veil-formalities.md) addresses veil piercing generally but does not adequately address the heightened risks for single-member LLCs (SMLLCs). Courts have found it easier to pierce SMLLC veils, requiring additional protective measures.

## Key Findings

### Heightened Risk for Single-Member LLCs

Courts have found it easier to find a single member liable when the SMLLC is merely an alter ego for the individual, particularly:
- When involving unpaid debts of the SMLLC
- When the SMLLC engaged in unscrupulous, harmful acts
- When there is no separation between member and entity

```
CRITICAL INSIGHT: SMLLCs face HIGHER piercing risk
├─ No other members to enforce formalities
├─ Easier to prove unity of interest
├─ Commingling more common with single owner
├─ Less documentation typically maintained
└─ Some states more aggressive with SMLLCs
```

### The Two-Part Test

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│               VEIL PIERCING TEST (SMLLC)                            │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│                                                                     │
│  PRONG 1: UNITY OF INTEREST                                        │
│  ├─ SMLLC is not really separate from sole member                  │
│  ├─ Business and personal affairs commingled                       │
│  ├─ Corporate formalities not observed                             │
│  └─ Entity used as mere instrumentality                            │
│                                                                     │
│  PRONG 2: FRAUD & INJUSTICE                                        │
│  ├─ Sole member used SMLLC for fraud/unscrupulous acts             │
│  ├─ OR adherence to fiction would sanction injustice               │
│  ├─ Creditors would be unfairly harmed                             │
│  └─ Inequitable result if veil not pierced                         │
│                                                                     │
│  WHEN BOTH PRONGS MET → VEIL LIKELY PIERCED                        │
│                                                                     │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### 2024 Landmark Case: Ene v. Graham

The Nevada Supreme Court case Ene v. Graham, 546 P.3d 1232 (2024) is significant for SMLLC veil piercing:

```
CASE SUMMARY:

Facts:
├─ Laura Graham injured on property owned by International Property
│   Holdings, LLC ("IPH")
├─ Ovidiu Ene was the sole member of IPH
└─ Plaintiff sought to pierce veil to reach Ene personally

Significance:
├─ Addresses alter ego liability for property-owning SMLLCs
├─ Clarifies Nevada's three-part test
├─ Important precedent for real estate holding SMLLCs
└─ Personal injury context (involuntary creditor)
```

### Common Factors Leading to Piercing

```
MOST COMMON PIERCING FACTORS:

1. INADEQUATE CAPITALIZATION
   ├─ Insufficient initial capital for intended operations
   ├─ No retained earnings or reserves
   ├─ Member extracts all profits
   └─ Cannot meet foreseeable obligations

2. COMMINGLING OF ASSETS
   ├─ Business funds used for personal expenses
   ├─ Personal funds deposited to business account
   ├─ No clear distinction in financial records
   └─ Single account for both purposes

3. FRAUDULENT TRANSACTIONS
   ├─ Transfers to avoid creditors
   ├─ Shell game with assets
   ├─ Intentional undercapitalization
   └─ Deceptive representations to creditors

4. LACK OF FORMALITIES
   ├─ No operating agreement (or ignored)
   ├─ No meeting minutes or resolutions
   ├─ No separate business records
   └─ No arm's length contracts with member

5. LACK OF SEPARATION
   ├─ Using LLC assets as personal property
   ├─ Not maintaining LLC identity in dealings
   ├─ Personal guarantees without documentation
   └─ Unclear who is acting (member vs LLC)
```

### State Variations

| State | Standard | Key Considerations |
|-------|----------|-------------------|
| **California** | Alter ego + inequitable result | Unity of interest strictly interpreted; aggressive toward SMLLCs |
| **Delaware** | Fraud or similar injustice | Generally protective; requires more than formality failure |
| **Texas** | Actual fraud required | Higher bar; specific intent to deceive needed |
| **Nevada** | Three-part test | Influence, unity of interest, fraud/injustice |
| **Florida** | Alter ego + improper conduct | Focused on improper purpose |
| **New York** | Domination + wrongful conduct | Control plus harm required |

```
STATE-SPECIFIC RISKS:

California (HIGH RISK):
├─ Courts historically aggressive
├─ Alter ego doctrine broadly applied
├─ Personal injury claims get sympathy
└─ SMLLC structure viewed skeptically

Delaware (LOWER RISK):
├─ Generally protective of entity form
├─ Requires more than formality failure
├─ "Fraud or similar injustice" high bar
└─ Strong internal affairs doctrine

Texas (LOWER RISK):
├─ "Actual fraud" required for piercing
├─ Specific intent to deceive must be shown
├─ Mere formality failure insufficient
└─ BUT: Statutory requirements must be met
```

### Special SMLLC Risks

```
MEMBER-MANAGED SMLLC RISKS:
├─ No separation between management and ownership
├─ All decisions made by same person
├─ Higher scrutiny of formality compliance
├─ Risk of blurring personal and business
└─ Must be MORE careful, not less

SERIES LLC CONSIDERATIONS:
├─ Each series should be treated as separate
├─ Asset and liability segregation critical
├─ Commingling between series destroys protection
├─ Some states don't recognize series structure
└─ Banking complications (separate accounts needed)

HOLDING COMPANY SMLLC:
├─ Single member is often another entity
├─ Corporate member may help (vs individual)
├─ BUT: Can still be pierced if formalities ignored
├─ Upstream piercing possible
└─ Document intercompany transactions
```

### Protection Strategies

```
SMLLC PROTECTION CHECKLIST:

Formation:
☐ Written Operating Agreement (even if not required)
☐ Adequate initial capitalization
☐ Separate EIN/tax ID
☐ Registered agent designated
☐ Clear business purpose documented

Banking:
☐ Separate bank account(s)
☐ Business credit card (not personal)
☐ No commingling of funds
☐ Document capital contributions
☐ Document distributions properly

Operations:
☐ Sign all contracts in LLC capacity
☐ Use LLC name on all documents
☐ Maintain separate business records
☐ Keep personal and business receipts separate
☐ Pay LLC debts from LLC accounts

Governance:
☐ Annual resolutions/consents
☐ Document major decisions in writing
☐ Maintain minutes (even if informal)
☐ Review and update operating agreement
☐ File required annual reports

Insurance:
☐ Adequate liability insurance
☐ Named insured is the LLC
☐ Coverage appropriate to risk
☐ Professional liability if applicable
```

### Involuntary Creditors

```
SPECIAL CONSIDERATION: TORT CLAIMANTS

Involuntary creditors (tort victims) often receive more sympathy:
├─ Did not choose to deal with the LLC
├─ Could not assess LLC's creditworthiness
├─ Courts more willing to pierce
└─ Especially true for personal injury claims

EXAMPLE SCENARIO:
├─ SMLLC owns rental property
├─ Tenant injured due to negligence
├─ SMLLC has no insurance and minimal assets
├─ Court more likely to pierce to reach member
└─ Inadequate capitalization especially damaging

PROTECTION:
├─ Maintain adequate insurance
├─ Capitalize appropriately for risk
├─ Consider umbrella policy
└─ Document risk management efforts
```

## Cross-Domain Integration Requirements

### Entity Governance → Veil Protection
- Operating agreement must be robust
- Annual compliance checklist
- Board/member resolutions for major decisions
- Clear authority documentation

### Accounting → Evidence of Separation
- Separate books and records essential
- Clear capital contribution tracking
- Distribution documentation
- No personal expenses through LLC

### Banking → Financial Separation
- Dedicated bank accounts
- No commingling under any circumstances
- Proper signatory authority
- Document all intercompany transfers
