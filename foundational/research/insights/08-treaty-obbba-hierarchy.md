# The Treaty Override Question: OBBBA vs Existing Tax Treaties

> Curiosity #8: When OBBBA provisions conflict with existing tax treaties, which prevails? Do treaties protect against the new rules?

## The Question

OBBBA 2025 made significant changes to international tax rules (GILTI→NCTI, FTC modifications, pro-rata rules). Research mentioned treaty benefits but didn't address: When these statutory changes conflict with existing treaties, which wins?

## Key Findings

### The Later-in-Time Rule: U.S. Doctrine

```
U.S. TREATY OVERRIDE DOCTRINE:

The "Later-in-Time" Rule (Chae Chan Ping, 1889):

┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  In U.S. law, treaties and statutes have EQUAL status   │
│                                                         │
│  When they conflict:                                    │
│  ├─ The LATER in time prevails                          │
│  ├─ A later statute CAN override an earlier treaty      │
│  └─ A later treaty CAN override an earlier statute      │
│                                                         │
│  This is UNIQUE to the U.S.                             │
│  Most countries give treaties superiority               │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

CONSEQUENCE:
├─ OBBBA (2025) is LATER than most U.S. tax treaties
├─ To the extent OBBBA conflicts with treaties...
├─ OBBBA provisions can override treaty protections
└─ This is intentional in some cases, incidental in others
```

### Where Treaties MAY Provide Protection

```
TREATY PROVISIONS THAT MAY STILL HELP:

1. NON-DISCRIMINATION (Article 24 OECD Model)
   ├─ Foreign subsidiaries of U.S. companies cannot be
   │   treated WORSE than domestic companies
   ├─ May limit some OBBBA applications
   └─ But: GILTI/NCTI applied to all CFCs uniformly

2. LIMITATION ON BENEFITS (LOB)
   ├─ Treaties limit benefits to "qualified residents"
   ├─ OBBBA doesn't directly conflict with LOB
   └─ But: May interact with anti-treaty-shopping rules

3. COMPETENT AUTHORITY PROCEDURES
   ├─ Treaties provide dispute resolution
   ├─ Can request MAP for double taxation
   └─ But: Takes years; doesn't prevent initial application

4. SPECIFIC TREATY OVERRIDES
   ├─ Some treaties explicitly preserve domestic anti-abuse
   ├─ "Savings clause" allows U.S. to tax its citizens/residents
   └─ Most treaties already contemplate CFC-type rules
```

### OBBBA Provisions Most Likely to Create Treaty Tension

From [Steptoe Analysis](https://www.steptoe.com/en/news-publications/international-tax-changes-in-the-one-big-beautiful-bill-act.html):

```
AREAS OF POTENTIAL TREATY CONFLICT:

1. NCTI (FORMERLY GILTI) MODIFICATIONS
   ├─ Reduced deduction (40% vs 50%)
   ├─ Higher effective rate (12.6% vs 10.5%)
   ├─ Potential conflict: Treaty limits on taxing
   │   income before distribution
   └─ BUT: Most treaties have CFC carve-outs

2. FOREIGN TAX CREDIT CHANGES
   ├─ Section 960(d)(1): 90% credit (was 80%)
   ├─ New Section 960(d)(4): 10% haircut on PTEP credits
   ├─ Potential conflict: Treaties guarantee credit for
   │   foreign taxes paid
   └─ BUT: FTC limitations historically upheld

3. PRO-RATA SHARE RULES
   ├─ "At any time during year" ownership rule
   ├─ Potential conflict: Treaties define "resident"
   │   based on year-end status sometimes
   └─ BUT: This is domestic characterization, not treaty issue

4. DOWNWARD ATTRIBUTION CHANGES
   ├─ Modified rules for non-U.S. entity attribution
   ├─ Potential conflict: May create "CFC" status not
   │   anticipated by treaty partners
   └─ BUT: Attribution is U.S. domestic law construct
```

### The FTC Interaction

From [PWC Analysis](https://www.pwc.com/us/en/services/tax/library/treasury-releases-3-international-tax-notices-related-to-obbba.html):

```
FOREIGN TAX CREDIT UNDER OBBBA:

Prior to OBBBA:
├─ Section 960(d)(1): 80% of deemed-paid FTC allowed
├─ 20% haircut on NCTI-related foreign taxes
└─ Effective FTC = 80%

After OBBBA:
├─ Section 960(d)(1): 90% of deemed-paid FTC allowed
├─ 10% haircut on NCTI-related foreign taxes
├─ NEW Section 960(d)(4): Additional 10% haircut on
│   PTEP distributions after June 28, 2025
└─ Effective FTC = 90% on inclusions, less on PTEP

TREATY PERSPECTIVE:
├─ Treaties generally require allowing FTC for foreign taxes
├─ But: U.S. has historically imposed limitations
├─ Courts have upheld reasonable FTC limitations
└─ 10% haircut likely survives treaty challenge
```

### What Treaties Don't Protect Against

```
LIMITS OF TREATY PROTECTION:

1. CFC REGIMES SPECIFICALLY CONTEMPLATED
   ├─ Most modern treaties acknowledge CFC rules exist
   ├─ OECD commentary supports CFC regimes
   └─ NCTI/GILTI within this framework

2. DOMESTIC CHARACTERIZATION
   ├─ Whether income is "Subpart F" vs "NCTI"
   ├─ How to calculate pro-rata shares
   ├─ Attribution rules
   └─ These are U.S. internal matters

3. FTC LIMITATIONS (Within Reason)
   ├─ Treaties require credit, not unlimited credit
   ├─ Limitations based on baskets, timing, amounts
   └─ Historically upheld unless clearly discriminatory

4. PILLAR TWO INTERACTION
   ├─ Global minimum tax (15%) is treaty-compatible
   ├─ OBBBA's higher NCTI rate (12.6%) below Pillar Two
   └─ No conflict: U.S. rate lower than global minimum
```

### Treaty Shopping Considerations

From [Holland & Knight Analysis](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2025/07/a-look-at-the-international-tax-changes-in-the-obbb-act):

```
OBBBA AND TREATY SHOPPING:

OBBBA INTENT:
├─ Close loopholes used to avoid U.S. tax
├─ Ensure U.S. taxation on CFC income
├─ Limit treaty-shopping structures

HOW THIS AFFECTS PLANNING:
├─ Pre-OBBBA: Could use day-before-sale planning
│   └─ Sell CFC before year-end, buyer takes inclusion
├─ Post-OBBBA: Pro-rata rules prevent this
│   └─ Both parties share inclusion burden

├─ Pre-OBBBA: Dividend "flushing" before sale
│   └─ Reduce E&P to minimize buyer's exposure
├─ Post-OBBBA: Transition rule limits this (2024-2026)
│   └─ Dividends must increase U.S. taxable income

TREATY BENEFIT IMPLICATIONS:
├─ Structure that relied on treaty timing rules
│   may no longer work as planned
├─ Must reevaluate holding structures
└─ Treaty benefits for dividends may be less valuable
```

### Practical Guidance

```
NAVIGATING OBBBA-TREATY INTERACTIONS:

STEP 1: IDENTIFY AFFECTED STRUCTURES
├─ Which entities involve treaty countries?
├─ Which benefit from specific treaty provisions?
└─ Which relied on timing rules changed by OBBBA?

STEP 2: ANALYZE SPECIFIC CONFLICTS
├─ Does OBBBA directly override a treaty provision?
├─ Or does it change domestic law the treaty references?
└─ The latter is usually NOT a treaty override

STEP 3: CONSIDER REMEDIES
├─ Competent Authority request (for double taxation)
├─ Restructuring to maintain benefits
├─ Accepting changed economics
└─ Challenge (expensive, uncertain, long timeline)

STEP 4: DOCUMENT POSITION
├─ Why you believe treaty applies
├─ Why OBBBA doesn't override
├─ Reasonable basis for position
└─ Consider disclosure
```
